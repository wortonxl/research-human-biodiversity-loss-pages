The single most important, authoritative source establishing that human activity has caused biodiversity loss and ecosystem deterioration is the IPBES Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (2019) — the first fully intergovernmental global assessment of nature since the Millennium Ecosystem Assessment (2005). Produced by the Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES), commonly called the "IPCC for biodiversity," the report represents a consensus of 145 expert authors from 50 countries synthesizing roughly 15,000 scientific and indigenous/local knowledge sources, with its Summary for Policymakers approved line-by-line by 132 member governments.

Its central, empirically supported conclusion: biodiversity is declining faster than at any time in human history, ~1 million species face extinction within decades, and the five dominant drivers of this crisis are all anthropogenic. No competing source (WWF Living Planet Report, UNEP GEO) matches its combination of intergovernmental mandate, expert consensus, government approval, breadth of coverage, and direct causal attribution of human drivers.

Institutional Authority and Mandate (The "IPCC for Biodiversity")

  • IPBES is an independent intergovernmental body established by governments in 2012, comprising over 130 member nations — explicitly modeled as the biodiversity equivalent of the IPCC. IPBES Global Assessment Portal

  • The Global Assessment is the first fully government-authorised global assessment of biodiversity and ecosystem services since the Millennium Ecosystem Assessment (2005). Springer — The IPBES Global Assessment

  • Authored by 145 leading international experts from 50 countries, with inputs from an additional 310 experts, synthesizing approximately 15,000 scientific, government, and indigenous/local knowledge sources. IPBES Media Release — Global Assessment

  • Full report comprises six chapters totaling 1,148 pages, edited by co-chairs E. S. Brondizio, J. Settele, S. Díaz, and H. T. Ngo. IPBES Global Assessment (GA1)

Species Extinction Crisis (~1 Million Species at Risk)

  • An estimated 1 million species — approximately 25% of assessed plant and animal groups — face extinction, many within decades, unless drivers are reduced. (Corroborated: grok + perplexity) IPBES Media Release — Global Assessment

  • Global species extinction rates are already tens to hundreds of times higher than the natural background rate over the past 10 million years, and accelerating. IPBES Media Release — Global Assessment

  • More than 40% of amphibian species, ~33% of reef-forming corals, and more than a third of marine mammals are threatened. APO — Global Assessment summary

  • Biomass inversion: wild mammal biomass has fallen by 82%, while humans and domestic livestock now account for roughly 96% of all mammalian biomass on Earth. IPBES SPM — Official PDF

  • Average abundance of native species in most major terrestrial biomes has fallen by at least 20%, mostly since 1900. APO — Global Assessment summary

Degradation of Terrestrial, Marine, and Freshwater Ecosystems

  • 75% of the land surface has been significantly altered by human actions. (Corroborated: grok + perplexity) IPBES SPM — Official PDF

  • 66% of the marine/ocean environment has been significantly altered by cumulative human pressures (overfishing, pollution, coastal development). (Corroborated: grok + perplexity) IPBES SPM — Official PDF

  • Over 85% of wetlands present in 1700 have been lost/destroyed by 2000, with loss rates accelerating. (Corroborated: grok + perplexity) IPBES SPM — Official PDF

  • Land degradation has reduced productivity on 23% of global terrestrial area, risking hundreds of billions of dollars in annual crop output. (Corroborated: grok + perplexity) UNCCD — Global Assessment Report library

  • Urban areas have more than doubled since 1992; plastic pollution has increased tenfold since 1980. UNCCD — Global Assessment Report library

Nature's Contributions to People (Ecosystem Services Deterioration)

  • 14 of 18 categories of Nature's Contributions to People (NCP) — mostly regulating and non-material contributions — have declined since 1970. IISD SDG Knowledge Hub

  • Negative trends undermine progress toward ~80% of assessed SDG targets (especially SDGs 1, 2, 3, 6, 11, 13, 14, 15). IISD SDG Knowledge Hub

  • Most Aichi Biodiversity Targets for 2020 were missed, and only "transformative change" across economic, social, political, and technological factors can reverse current trajectories. IISD SDG Knowledge Hub

All five drivers are anthropogenic in origin and ranked by descending global aggregate impact. (Ranking corroborated identically by both grok + perplexity.)

1. Changes in Land and Sea Use

  • The dominant driver for terrestrial and freshwater systems; agriculture alone uses >1/3 of the land surface and ~75% of freshwater. Driven by agricultural expansion, urban sprawl, logging, and infrastructure. IPBES SPM — Official PDF Victoria CES — IPBES Global Assessment

2. Direct Exploitation of Organisms

  • The second largest global driver and the primary driver in marine environments — overfishing, destructive trawling, hunting, poaching, logging, and uncontrolled harvesting. IPBES-7 Working Document

3. Climate Change

  • Anthropogenic GHG emissions driving habitat shifts, ocean acidification, more frequent extreme weather, and shifting biome envelopes; it exacerbates all other drivers, acting from genes to ecosystems. IPBES SPM — Official PDF

4. Pollution

  • Atmospheric, terrestrial, and marine pollutants — marine plastic up tenfold since 1980, untreated heavy metals, industrial toxic waste, and agricultural nutrient runoff creating hundreds of hypoxic "dead zones." IPBES Media Release — Global Assessment

5. Invasive Alien Species

  • Global transport and trade vectors introducing exotic plants, animals, and pathogens that disrupt native ecosystems and outcompete endemic taxa. IPBES SPM — Official PDF

Indirect (root) drivers that the report identifies as underlying the five direct drivers:

  • Demographic & consumption growth: global human population has doubled since 1970, with per-capita consumption and global trade increasing multi-fold, driving resource extraction. IPBES SPM — Official PDF

  • Perverse subsidies & economic incentives: financial systems and subsidies reward environmentally harmful activities (fossil fuels, destructive agriculture, industrial fisheries) and fail to account for externalities. IPBES Media Release — Global Assessment EU Knowledge4Policy

  • Ineffective governance & enforcement deficits: fragmented policy, weak environmental rule of law, and failure to account for natural capital in GDP/national accounting. IPBES SPM — Official PDF

  • Technological innovations that increase resource extraction and trade intensity (indirect driver identified by grok). EU Knowledge4Policy

Debate card — Aff (proves human causation): - Intergovernmental consensus of 132 governments = near-unassailable authority, cannot be dismissed as NGO alarmism. - "Human actions threaten more species with global extinction now than ever before" — direct causal attribution. - Five direct drivers, all anthropogenic, ranked by measured global impact.

Debate card — Neg (limits/caveats): - Assessment is descriptive, not prescriptive — does not mandate specific policy, so it does not prove any particular solution. - The ~1 million figure is an estimate for species threatened, not confirmed extinctions. - The report itself states transformative change is still achievable — which undercuts any doomsday/fatalistic framing.
